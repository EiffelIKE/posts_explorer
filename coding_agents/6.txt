● Both cases already work as you described, and I didn't change any code. I
  added them to the plan's testing step.

  How I checked: I wrote a temporary test against the real UserPostsRoute,
  holding back the POST /posts response with MSW. All 3 checks passed:

  1. Late response after switching user: I published as Leanne, switched to
     Ervin, then let the server answer. Ervin's panel showed neither the post
     nor the "Published" message. Going back to Leanne, the post was there.
     - Why it works: onSuccess in useCreatePost(userId) belongs to the request
       itself, so it still runs after the panel closes. It still holds Leanne's
       ID, so it only writes to ['local-posts', 1]. The panel is keyed by user,
       so Ervin gets a new composer and only reads his own posts.
  2. Switch away and back: I created a post as Leanne, switched to Ervin (the
     post wasn't there), then went back to Leanne (it was).
     - Only for that session: a fresh query client, which is what a reload gives
       you, starts with no created posts. They live only in memory, and the
       never-expire setting from decision 12 keeps them until the tab reloads.

  - New decision 17 explains why they work and lists the known limit below.
